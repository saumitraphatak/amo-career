The Records Board

The best published numbers for each quantum computing platform, in one place: gate fidelities, system size, coherence and error correction. Every entry names its paper and date and says how much to trust it: peer-reviewed, preprint, or company-reported. Records measured on one pair of qubits and averages over a whole machine are both here, and labelled as such, because they answer different questions.

How to read a record

peer-reviewedPublished in a refereed journal.
preprintOn arXiv, not yet refereed. Often right, sometimes revised.
company-reportedA press release, blog or product sheet, without an independent paper behind the number.
one pairMeasured on one or a few qubit pairs, not averaged over a whole processor.
conditionalCounts only the runs where nothing was lost (post-selected or heralded), so loss is not in the number.

How entries are chosen

  • Each card is the best number we could verify against the paper or the company's own statement, as of the date at the top. When the best preprint and the best peer-reviewed result differ, both are listed.
  • Numbers are copied as published, including uncertainties where given. Fidelities from different benchmarks (randomized benchmarking, cross-entropy, Bell-state fidelity) are not strictly interchangeable.
  • Physical-qubit counts are atoms or qubits held and controlled in one system; they say nothing on their own about gate quality.
  • Something missing or out of date? Use "Report an error" at the bottom of the page. Records move fast, and corrections are welcome.
  • For context and the full history behind each platform, see the comparison chapters: Rb vs Yb, Quantinuum vs IonQ, Google vs IBM, PsiQuantum vs Xanadu and the QC landscape.
